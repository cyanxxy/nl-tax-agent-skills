## Social insurance

Mandatory Dutch national-insurance coverage and income-tax liability are
different questions. Emigration may end coverage, but continued work in the
Netherlands, posting or another special situation can preserve it. The
emigration guidance asks for the period of mandatory insurance and describes
time-proportional premium parts of tax credits. It also distinguishes
voluntary AOW/Anw coverage and the CAK overseas healthcare contribution.

Collect actual country/work periods, the scheme, whether coverage was
mandatory or voluntary, and the controlling SVB/A1/posting or authority
evidence. Do not infer coverage or premium reduction merely from residence,
Dutch income, a withholding line or the qualifying-income test. Mixed-country
work and disputed scheme/date treatment require specialist review. This
summary supplies no complete premium or healthcare-contribution formula.

## Country of income and relief

A Netherlands resident generally reports worldwide income. A nonresident's
Dutch tax exposure concerns Dutch income/assets, subject to the applicable
treaty: the Netherlands may lack the taxing right for a particular item.
Reporting income and ultimately paying Dutch tax on it are separate.

For each group collect residence/source countries, physical work location
where relevant, category, dates, gross amounts and separate foreign/Dutch
tax withheld. Confirm the exact treaty/version/article and relief method
through official authority or adviser evidence. The general page does not
decide a particular country's treaty outcome. Do not automatically apply a
day-count exemption, treat foreign withholding as a credit, remove reported
worldwide income, choose treaty residence or calculate an exemption/credit.

An unresolved residence tie-breaker, permanent establishment, pension,
government service or special income item remains a treatment blocker while
unaffected evidence collection continues. A tax treaty does not by itself
settle social-insurance coverage.
